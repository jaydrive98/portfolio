Digital Dialogues
Curated Webinar Registration

An event-driven integration for invite-only webinars. Applicants sign up on a form, their details flow straight into the CRM with their marketing consent, and — once the team has hand-picked who's in — approved guests are registered into the Microsoft Teams webinar automatically. No spreadsheets, no manual re-typing.

The problem

A guest list the team wanted to curate by hand

"Digital Dialogues" are small, high-value online sessions — and the team deliberately chose not to let just anyone in. They wanted to review each applicant and hand-pick the room. That's easy to say and painful to run by hand: collect applications, capture marketing consent properly, decide who's approved, then get each accepted person correctly registered into the Microsoft Teams webinar — all without data being re-typed between four different tools.

The goal was to make that whole journey automatic and consent-safe: one form to apply, the CRM always up to date, and a simple approve/reject action that quietly does everything downstream.

How it works

When someone applies, CognitoForms fires a webhook to a Python Cloud Run function. It upserts the applicant into HubSpot (so no duplicates) and sets their email subscriptions based on the consent they gave on the form. Nobody is in the webinar yet — they're just a known, consented contact.

The team then approves or rejects each applicant right inside CognitoForms. That decision fires a second webhook carrying the acceptance status. Only if the status is approved does the function call the Microsoft Graph API to register that person into the Teams webinar. Rejections simply stop at the gate — no registration, no email.

Engineering highlights

The interesting bits

Webhook-driven & serverless

A single stateless Cloud Run function handles both webhook events — nothing to keep running between sessions, and it scales to whatever a registration burst throws at it.

Consent captured at source

Marketing-subscription consent is taken on the application form itself and written straight into HubSpot's communication preferences — so the CRM's permission state is always truthful.

Fuzzy subscription matching

The form's subscription field names rarely match HubSpot's exactly. A Levenshtein-distance match maps each form option to the closest real HubSpot subscription type, so a small wording drift never breaks consent.

Idempotent contact upserts

Contacts go in via HubSpot's batch upsert, keyed on email — an applicant who submits twice updates cleanly instead of creating duplicates.

Approval as a hard gate

Teams registration is strictly downstream of an approval. The acceptance status in the second webhook is the only thing that unlocks it, keeping the "invite-only" promise enforced in code.

Microsoft Graph virtual events

Approved guests are registered through the Microsoft Graph virtual-events API against the specific Teams webinar, using an OAuth client-credentials token — no manual Teams admin steps.

The result

Hand-picked guests, hands-off logistics

The team gets exactly what they wanted: full control over who attends, with none of the busywork. Applicants flow into the CRM the moment they sign up, consent and all; approvals take one click; and accepted guests land in the Teams webinar without anyone copying a name or email by hand.
